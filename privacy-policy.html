1. Introduction

Welcome to EchoDrop ("we," "our," or "us"). We are committed to protecting your privacy and ensuring the security of your personal information. This Privacy Policy explains how we collect, use, disclose, and safeguard your information when you use our mobile application and related services (collectively, the "Service").

Who is responsible for your data: EchoDrop is the controller of the personal data described in this policy. For anything about your personal data, email privacy@getechodrop.com.

By using EchoDrop, you agree to the collection and use of information in accordance with this policy.

2. Information We Collect

2.1 Information You Provide

  • Account Information: When you create an account, we collect your display name, username, email address, and profile photo.
  • Profile Information: Bio, memoji/avatar, and preferences you choose to share.
  • User Content: Posts, photos, videos, comments, reactions, and other content you create within the app.
  • Tribe Information: Data related to tribes you create or join, including tribe names, descriptions, cover photos, and membership.
  • Communications: Messages sent through direct messaging and tribe channels.
  • Contacts (only if you use Find Friends): Codes made on your phone from the email addresses in your contacts, as explained in section 2.4.
  • Your country when you first give your age: When you first tell us your age group, we record the two-letter code of the country your device's region is set to (for example 'IN'), and where that code came from. We use it only to apply that country's minimum age and to keep a record that we did. We do not use it for advertising or recommendations, and we never show it to anyone else. Our lawful basis is our legitimate interest in keeping people below the minimum age off EchoDrop, and our legal obligations where a law requires age checks. It is stored with Supabase in the UK. We keep it while your account exists and delete it with your account. It is included when you download your data.

2.2 Information Collected Automatically

  • Device Information: Device type, operating system, unique device identifiers, and mobile network information.
  • Usage Data: How you interact with the Service, including features used, content viewed, and time spent.
  • Location Data: With your permission, we may collect precise or approximate location data to enable location-based features such as the Vibe Map, checking in to a place, and local weather in Guides. Section 2.3 explains which services receive your coordinates.

2.3 Third-Party Services

  • Music Services: If you connect Spotify or Apple Music, we may access playlist and listening data to power music-based features. We do not store your streaming credentials.
  • Authentication: If you sign in via third-party providers (e.g., Apple, Google), we receive basic profile information as permitted by your settings.
  • AI Services: EchoDrop uses artificial intelligence to power certain features. When you use AI-powered features (such as the Guide, mood-based suggestions, or content recommendations), your prompts and relevant context may be processed by the following third-party AI providers:
    • Cloudflare Workers AI: Primary AI provider for text analysis and recommendations. Data is processed in transit and not retained after the response is generated.
    • Groq: Fallback AI provider for complex reasoning tasks. Data is processed in transit and subject to Groq's data processing terms.
    We do not send your personal profile information, messages, or location data to AI providers. Only the specific content you submit to AI-powered features is transmitted. No AI provider is used for advertising or user profiling.
  • Content Safety: Uploaded images are checked for nudity by an image safety model that EchoDrop runs itself, on our own backend, which Supabase hosts for us. The model is our own copy of an openly published model, so images are not sent to any other company for this check.
  • Place Names and Map Search (Nominatim): When you use the Vibe Map or check in to a place, the app sends your location coordinates directly from your phone to Nominatim, the place-name service run by the OpenStreetMap Foundation, to look up the name of the place (reverse geocoding). When you search the map, the app sends the words you search for. The app rounds these coordinates to two decimal places (roughly 1 km) before sending them, so Nominatim learns your approximate area, not your exact position. We do not send your name, username or account details, but because the request comes from your phone, Nominatim also receives your device's IP address. The OpenStreetMap Foundation's privacy policy is at osmfoundation.org/wiki/Privacy_Policy.
  • Map images (OpenStreetMap and Esri): To draw the Vibe Map, your phone downloads map images for the area on screen from OpenStreetMap's tile servers, or from Esri for satellite view. These requests show the area you are viewing and your device's IP address, but not your name or account details.
  • Weather (Open-Meteo): If you use Guides, the app asks your phone for a low-accuracy location and sends those coordinates, rounded to two decimal places (roughly 1 km), directly from your phone to Open-Meteo to get your local weather. If your phone cannot work out the name of your town itself, the app may also send the same rounded coordinates to Open-Meteo's place-name service. We do not send your name, username or account details, but Open-Meteo receives your device's IP address.
  • GIF search (KLIPY): When you search for a GIF, the app sends the words you search for, with a strict content filter, directly from your phone to KLIPY to find matching GIFs. We do not send your name, username or account details, but KLIPY receives your device's IP address.
  • Product Analytics (off unless you turn it on): We use PostHog (EU hosting, eu.i.posthog.com) to count how the app is used, such as which screens open and where things go wrong, so we can improve it. Nothing is counted unless you say yes: the app asks adults once, and you can change your answer at any time in Settings → Privacy Settings → Share app usage statistics. Turning it off stops it straight away, and anything not yet sent is thrown away. We only count people we have confirmed are 18 or over, so nothing is counted for anyone under 18, or for anyone whose age we have not confirmed, whatever they answer. The counts are not linked to your account: we do not send PostHog your account's identifier, your email address, name or username, your messages, what you write or post, your mood or your location, and the app does not keep an identifier for PostHog from one visit to the next. Each event carries only its name, short labels and counts, your app version, and technical details such as your phone's operating system. PostHog receives your device's IP address with each request, but we have turned off its location lookup. PostHog acts as our service provider and does not use the data for its own purposes or for advertising. With the same answer, Sentry (see Crash Reporting below) also counts how many visits to the app end in a crash. It does this in the United States, and these counts are not linked to your account either. We keep a record of your answer, and when you gave it, with your account, so that we can show what you agreed to.
  • Crash Reporting: We use Sentry for crash reporting and performance monitoring. Crash data and performance metrics are sent to Sentry's servers and are not linked to your identity.

2.4 Contacts and Find Friends

Find Friends is optional. If you use it and allow EchoDrop to read your contacts, your phone turns each email address in your contacts into a one-way code (a "hash") before anything is sent. Your address book itself never leaves your phone, and we do not read or send phone numbers. On our backend, which Supabase hosts for us, we compare the codes with the codes of adult EchoDrop accounts that have not turned off Discoverable by Contacts, show you any matches, and throw the codes away straight after matching. We keep only how many codes were checked and how many matched, with your account. Nobody under 18 can be found through Find Friends.

If your email address is in someone's contacts, a code made from it may pass through our systems for a moment in this way. We cannot contact you, and we keep nothing that identifies you. A code like this can sometimes be matched back to an email address by someone who already has a list of addresses, which is why we delete the codes at once.

Being found: if you are 18 or over, people who have your email address in their contacts can find your account through Find Friends. This is on by default. You can turn it off at any time in Settings → Privacy Settings → Discoverable by Contacts.

Our lawful basis is your consent for reading your contacts, which you can withdraw at any time in your iPhone's settings, and our legitimate interests in helping people find people they know, for the codes made from your contacts' email addresses.

We use the information we collect for the purposes below. Data protection law requires us to have a legal reason, called a "lawful basis", for each one. The lawful basis we rely on is shown under each purpose.

  • Provide, maintain, and improve the Service, including the location features you choose to use
    Lawful basis: to provide the Service you signed up for (performance of our contract with you). If you are under 18, we rely on our legitimate interests in providing the Service you asked for instead. Improving the Service relies on our legitimate interests in making EchoDrop work better.
  • Personalise your experience, including content recommendations and the discovery feed
    Lawful basis: our legitimate interests in showing you content you are likely to find relevant.
  • Enable social features such as tribes, posts, reactions, and messaging
    Lawful basis: performance of our contract with you. If you are under 18: our legitimate interests in providing the Service you asked for.
  • Send you notifications related to your account and activity
    Lawful basis: performance of our contract with you. If you are under 18: our legitimate interests in providing the Service you asked for. You can turn notifications off in your device or app settings.
  • Detect, investigate, and prevent fraud or abuse
    Lawful basis: our legitimate interests, and those of other users, in keeping EchoDrop safe, and our legal obligations where the law requires us to act.
  • Comply with legal obligations
    Lawful basis: compliance with a legal obligation.
  • Conduct analytics to understand how our Service is used
    Lawful basis: your consent, which you give by saying yes to usage statistics. You can withdraw it at any time, and withdrawing does not affect counting done before then (see "Product Analytics" in section 2.3).

Where we rely on legitimate interests, you have the right to object (see section 6).

How we check images for illegal content

To keep EchoDrop safe, we check images in two ways.

In the app, before an image is posted or sent, including in a private message, we check whether it shows nudity, as described under "Content Safety" in section 2.3. The app also works out a 'fingerprint' of the image (a hash, a short code that cannot be turned back into the picture) and sends it to us. We compare it with our own list of images we have already found and removed as child sexual abuse material or as intimate images shared without consent.

On our servers, after an image is uploaded to a public or shared part of EchoDrop (posts, profiles, Hives, albums and similar), we make the same fingerprint comparison again. We do not run this server check on images in private messages.

If an image matches, we hide it straight away and review it ourselves before taking any further step. We report confirmed child sexual abuse material to the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children (NCMEC), which passes reports to the police in the country concerned, including the UK's National Crime Agency. Where UK law requires us to report to the National Crime Agency directly, we do. We keep a secure copy of reported material for at least 1 year, as the law requires, and review it before deleting it. If a match turns out to be wrong, we delete the copy at once. Only the founder can open these copies.

Our lawful basis is our legal obligations to tackle illegal content and our legitimate interest in protecting children and the people shown in intimate images. For private messages in the EU we rely on the EU's temporary rules for detecting child sexual abuse online (Regulation (EU) 2021/1232, as extended).

If we remove your content or restrict your account, we tell you why and how to appeal. You can also complain to your data protection authority or go to court.

8. Data Retention

We retain your information for as long as your account is active or as needed to provide the Service. Specific retention periods:

  • Account data (profile, email, preferences): Duration of account. It is deleted when your account is deleted, as set out below, except that we keep your email address only until the deletion has finished, and never more than 30 days.
  • Messages: Duration of conversation. Unsent messages are removed from view but retained for safety investigations for 90 days.
  • Capsules: Auto-expire after 24 hours, or until manually deleted
  • Content (posts, voice notes, articles): Duration of account or until user deletes
  • Safety data (moderation logs, contact rate logs): 90 days
  • Grooming/safety reports: 24 months (legal obligation for child protection)
  • Crash reports & diagnostics: 90 days
  • Analytics events: 12 months, after which they are deleted or aggregated so they no longer identify an individual account

Account Deletion Timeline

You can delete your account in the app, in Settings → Account Information → Delete account. When you ask:

  1. Days 1 to 7 (grace period): Your account is scheduled for deletion 7 days later, and the app shows you the date. Until then your account stays as it is, and you can cancel the deletion in the app at any time.
  2. After the 7 days: In our next nightly deletion run, we delete your account and the information linked to it from our database, including your profile, posts, capsules, messages, connections, likes, comments and other interactions, apart from the safety records described below.
  3. Shortly after that: We delete your files (photos, videos, voice notes and other uploads) from storage and ask PostHog to delete any analytics records that older versions of the app linked to your account. If you signed in with Apple, we also ask Apple to disconnect EchoDrop from your Apple Account (the app may ask you to confirm with Apple once so that we can).

A deletion can take longer while a safety report or a review about your account is open.

Copies made before the deletion can remain in our encrypted database backups. If we ever restore a backup, we delete those accounts again.

After deletion we keep only: reports and safety alerts that involve your account, including reports you made, and copies of the messages and posts they are about, for up to 12 months (24 months when a report is about a child's safety, and longer only while the law requires us to keep evidence); records of contact we stopped between an adult and someone under 18, for 12 months; a record that the account was deleted and when, for 2 years; your username for 60 days, so that nobody else can take it straight away; if the account was banned, one-way codes made from its email address and Apple or Google sign-in, for 3 years, so that the same person cannot simply sign up again; and analytics counts that were never linked to you.
